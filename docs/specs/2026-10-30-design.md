# Maritime AI showcase: design

The tool is named **Telltale** (chosen by Varun, 30 Oct 2026): the small indicator on board that warns you something is off before it goes wrong.
Owner: Varun Bhambri. Written 30 Oct 2026. **Approved by Varun 30 Oct 2026.**

## 1. What this is, and why

A briefing tool for officers on merchant ships. It answers two questions from published
records, and cites every claim:

1. **Before a job:** "What has gone wrong on other ships doing exactly this?"
   (an enclosed space entry, a bunkering, work on a purifier, a crane lift)
2. **Before a port call, or at any time:** "What do inspectors find and detain ships like ours for?"

It is built in public, one AI skill per stage, and each stage is posted on Varun's LinkedIn.
The skills are RAG, evals, guardrails, agents, LLM ops and fine-tuning. A ship-traffic (AIS)
chapter comes after those.

**Why it exists (Varun's words, summarised):** after 17 years at sea as a marine engineer
officer, he is moving into technology. In three months he wants recognition on LinkedIn from
the shipping community, his network, and tech-first ship managers and tech-first people in
shipping. Success is reputation, not users.

## 2. Users

First users are officers **on board**: Chief Engineer, Chief Officer, Master.
Office users (superintendents) come later, if at all.

## 3. How the ship works today (from Varun)

**Job preparation** already involves: the company SMS, standard laid-out procedures, the
maker's manual, permit to work, the safety management system, the PPE matrix, a toolbox
meeting, planning for major jobs, permission from the office, and risk assessment.

**Lessons from other ships** reach the crew through the monthly safety management committee
meeting (all crew, also used for fleet circulars from the office), an emergency safety
committee meeting called for anything urgent, and a toolbox meeting in the engine control room,
cargo control room or ship's office when a fleet broadcast or incident report arrives.

**What makes a lesson land:** the severity of the incident and the outcome that happened.
**What makes it go in one ear and out the other:** a casual attitude, and the extra paperwork
the company adds after an incident. The crew feel paperwork is an additional job.

**PSC preparation** starts in advance. The marine superintendent sends recommendations for the
port, the MOU and any concentrated inspection campaign, plus the most common findings; the
ship closes each one and sends photographs as proof. For inspections like the USCG, the
superintendent sails with the ship from the previous port. **Varun's decision:** the ship must
be ready for inspection at any time without waiting for the superintendent, so the tool helps
the ship prepare on its own.

## 4. Where the tool fits

It adds to two moments the ship already has. It never adds a meeting, a form or a step.

- **Job time:** the officer asks before the toolbox meeting and gets matching cases to talk through.
- **Meeting time:** a short pack for the monthly safety committee: recent cases that fit this
  ship's type, its upcoming jobs and its next ports, including the PSC picture.

## 5. Rules the tool must keep (goals)

0. **Never invent a fact (Varun, 30 Oct 2026: "a matter of ship safety and personal safety").**
   Above every other rule.
   - Every sentence in an answer must be backed by a passage in a cited source, and the
     passage is shown with it. The model adds nothing from its own general knowledge: no
     procedures, no figures, no regulation text it was not given.
   - An automatic check compares each sentence with the passage it cites. Any sentence the
     passage does not support is removed, and if too little is left, the tool says it has no
     reliable answer.
   - Where more than one source covers the same casualty or detention (for example an NTSB
     report and a USCG record), both are shown, and any disagreement is shown, not resolved.
   - Varun reads and approves every saved example answer before it is public, and every fact
     in every LinkedIn post is checked against its source first.

1. **Lead with what happened and how it ended.** Outcome first, in plain words, then how it
   happened, then what would have stopped it.
2. **No blame.** The investigation reports themselves are not for apportioning blame; neither is the tool.
3. **Cite or stay silent.** Every case and every fact links to the report or record it came from.
   If nothing matches, it says so.
4. **No new paperwork.** It never produces a form, a checklist to fill in, or anything to sign.
   Output is something an officer can say aloud in two to three minutes.
5. **Never says "safe to proceed"** or anything that stands in for the permit, the risk
   assessment, the SMS or office permission.

## 6. Non-goals and anti-goals

Non-goals: replacing the SMS, permit to work or risk assessment; serving the office first;
covering every regulation; selling anything during these three months.

Anti-goals (a failure even if it works):
- A screenshot of the tool giving a careless safety answer.
- Anything that reads as "AI-powered" hype.
- Naming or blaming a real crew member, or sensationalising a real casualty.
- Any data, wording or mention from past employers, clients, vessels sailed on, or SVYFT.
- Using IMO texts (IMO holds the copyright) or report photographs (some are third-party).

## 8. Stages

Roughly one stage every two weeks, about twelve weeks in all. Each stage ends with something
showable (a screenshot or short screen recording) and one real finding from the data that Varun
can stand behind.

1. **RAG.** Search the accident reports (MAIB, ATSB, NTSB) and return cited cases for a job.
2. **Evals.** Varun writes a question set with correct answers from the reports; the stage-1
   system is scored against it. Every later stage is measured on this set. The headline
   measure is the share of sentences not backed by their cited passage; the target is zero.
3. **Guardrails.** Enforce the rules in section 5, starting with rule 0. **The live public link
   opens at the end of this stage, and only if** the eval set shows no unsupported sentences and
   Varun has approved every saved example answer. It opens with a daily spend cap, a per-visitor question limit, and saved example
   questions whose answers can be viewed without a model call.
4. **Agents.** Add the PSC side from USCG data, and an agent that puts together the job-time
   briefing and the monthly safety-meeting pack.
5. **LLM ops.** Tracing of every model and tool call, cost and time per briefing, versioned
   prompts, and the eval set re-run on every change.
6. **Fine-tuning.** A small open-weights model that can run on board without a connection
   (ships' internet is thin and costly). Its result on the eval set is compared with the main
   model and published, whichever wins.
**Additions approved by Varun, 30 Oct 2026 ("this is great. we use them"):**
- **Stage 2 adds a public test set:** Varun's questions and answers published as an open maritime
  safety benchmark (questions, expected answers, source passages; no ship or company named beyond what
  the public reports name), with a simple table comparing how different AI models score on it. The
  held-out part stays private so it stays a fair test.
- **Stage 4 adds an MCP server:** the report search exposed through the Model Context Protocol, so
  other AI assistants can search the reports and get the same cited passages.

**Reputation steps approved by Varun, 30 Oct 2026 ("these are very much doable"):**
- **Build log** in the repo (`docs/build-log.md`): one dated entry per stage, in plain words: what
  worked, what failed, measured numbers, what it cost. Starts with the 30 Oct test.
- **"What it can't do" page** (`docs/limits.md`, linked from the tool's footer): its limits, its
  sources and what they don't cover, the measured share of unsupported sentences, and the rule that
  it never says "safe to proceed". Updated every stage.
- **Case study on Varun's portfolio website:** one page per stage, written for ship managers and
  technical leaders, linked from LinkedIn. Built in the portfolio project, not this repo.
- **Note to the report publishers** when the tool is ready (with the public link, end of stage 3):
  a short courtesy email to the MAIB, and later the ATSB, saying their reports are used, with credit
  and a link. Varun sends it himself.

7. **Later: AIS.** For example, replaying a ship's track before a US casualty next to the NTSB
   findings, with a careful tone.

## 9. Posting

Each stage is posted on LinkedIn. Posts lead with the ship problem and the skill comes second;
Varun's audit shows tech readers are under 5% of his viewers. The transition from sea to tech is
shown through the work, not stated. Detailed series plan: `~/.claude/linkedin/`.

## 10. Calls made by the writer (not yet confirmed by Varun)

- AI core in Python (evals and fine-tuning tooling); Claude as the main model, chosen per stage
  by cost and eval score; a simple web page for demos and the live link.
- The stage order above, with evals second.
- Fine-tuning approach (for example, training on briefings Varun has approved, or a smaller
  tagging model) is left to the builder, judged on the eval set.
- **The live link is a showpiece** for LinkedIn visitors and officers who want to try it. It is
  not a production tool for daily use on ships in these three months: no accounts, no saved
  ship profiles, no phone app.
- **"Ships like ours" is typed into the question** (ship type, next port, the job). No profile
  form. The saved example questions use a few fixed demo ships.
- **Offline target:** a CPU-only laptop in the ship's office, so a model of about 1 to 3 billion
  parameters. Search uses an embedding model that can run locally from stage 1, so the report
  index can later go on board without being rebuilt.
- **Eval set:** starts at about 30 questions for stage 2 and grows towards 150 before the
  fine-tuning comparison. A held-out part is kept from the start and is never used for training.
  Scoring covers both "right reports found" and "answer agrees with Varun's answer".

## 10a. Budget (Varun, 30 Oct 2026)

**$150 ceiling for the three months**, enforced with a spend limit in Anthropic's console.
Start lean on Claude Haiku 5.5 and move to Sonnet 5.5 or Opus 5.5 only where the eval set shows a
better score. Eval runs use the batch discount. The live link has a hard cap of $2 a day.
Hosting on free tiers (to confirm limits). Fine-tuning on a rented graphics card, about 10 to 20 hours.
Estimates behind this (writer's, from 6 Oct 2026 Claude prices and an assumed 20,000 tokens in and
1,300 out per checked briefing): about $50 lean, $300 on Sonnet, $550 or more on Opus. Stage 1
replaces these assumptions with measured figures.
