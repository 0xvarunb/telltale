How Telltale is being built

Telltale finds what went wrong on other ships for the job your ship is about to do, from published accident reports. I'm building it in public, and this page records each change: what I changed, why, and what it measured.

If it can't back a sentence up with a report, it shouldn't say it.

How I work

Each change follows the same four steps. I measure by running real questions, find the fault by checking each answer against the report page it cites, change one thing and write down why, then measure again with the same questions.

6 Nov 2026

The first real questions

Three questions an officer might ask before a job: entering a ballast tank on a bulk carrier, mooring alongside on a container ship, and lowering the lifeboat for a drill on a ro-ro ferry.

ChangeWhyResult
Judge a case by the job, not the ship typeThe search found a ballast tank entry on a container ship, where a chief officer fell almost 8 m. The answer said the reports had nothing, because the question named a bulk carrier. My own rule against stretching unrelated cases had made it treat the ship type as the job.The next run used the case and said what type of ship it was.
Tighter instructions for writing the answerAnswers had lead-ins, comments about the search results, points made twice and figure captions quoted as sentences, all without a source. One explained a lifeboat device from the AI's own knowledge, and got it wrong. Now every sentence must cite its passage and keep the report's own words for how sure it is, and nothing may be explained that the reports don't explain.Sentences with no source fell from 24 of 71 (34%) to 13 of 56 (23%). Cost fell from $0.0026 to $0.0022 per answer.
Stop tuning on these three questionsThe 23% comes from the same questions I tuned on, so it flatters the tool. Two runs of one question also gave 6 of 25 and 2 of 19 sentences with no source, so a single run proves little.A proper test set comes next, with some questions kept aside so I can't tune to them.
Still wrong
  • One answer cited the right page and still miscounted the report's recommendations. A citation alone doesn't make a sentence right.
  • About a quarter of sentences still have no source. The page marks them "no source", but doesn't yet remove them.
4–6 Nov 2026

Loading the full library

Every MAIB report on GOV.UK for merchant vessels of 100 gross tons or over: 635 entries.

ChangeWhyResult
Pick the full report, not the summaryThe first full load had 33 reports with no text and many with only a page or two. For 163 older reports it had picked a one-page synopsis, because GOV.UK gives no file sizes for them.Attachments are now ranked so the full report comes first.
Keep the other MAIB report types, labelledThe first load kept full investigations only, and left out safety bulletins and preliminary examinations that are useful to officers.Each case says what kind of report it is, so a bulletin is never taken for a full investigation. 619 kept; 16 overseas or discontinued left out.
Read scanned pagesSome older reports are pictures of pages, with no text a computer can read.1,248 pages read with OCR on the laptop. Every passage from a scan is marked, because OCR can misread a word or a figure.
Read the web page when there's no PDF180 reports had no PDF. They are preliminary examinations, which MAIB publishes as the text of the web page.All 619 reports searchable: 439 from PDFs, 180 from their web pages.
30 Oct 2026

The first test, and the first build

A small test on 21 MAIB reports, before building anything, to see whether an AI could brief an officer without inventing facts.

ChangeWhyResult
Measured the AI on its ownTo know the starting point.21 of 60 sentences it wrote had nothing in a report to back them up, about 1 in 3.
Checked every sentence against its passageTo catch what the AI adds.It flagged every unsupported sentence checked, and caught all 6 wrong details planted on purpose, such as a tank sounding changed from 50 cm to 80 cm. Each question cost less than one US cent to answer and check.
Built the first versionTo run it on the full set of reports.Search over the reports, answers that cite the report page for each sentence, and a "no source" mark on any sentence without one. It runs on a laptop.

The plan

Six stages, one AI skill each. No dates until a stage is measured.

  1. 1Search (RAG)Find the matching report passages and answer only from thembuilt
  2. 2Test set (evals)Questions with expected answers, from people who have done the jobsmeasured
  3. 3GuardrailsRemove any sentence its passage doesn't supportin progress
  4. 4BriefingsPort state control findings, the job briefing and the safety meeting packlater
  5. 5Running costsTrace each answer's cost and time, and re-test on every changelater
